💧 The Water Cycle
 How water moves between the oceans, the air, and the land
 Earth Science 
 Water is always on the move. The sun's energy drives a continuous loop that carries water from the surface into the atmosphere and back again. This loop is called the water cycle. Below are its three main stages.
 1. Evaporation
 Heat from the sun turns liquid water in oceans, lakes, and rivers into water vapor, an invisible gas that rises into the air. Plants also release vapor through their leaves, which is called transpiration.
 💡 Key idea Evaporation needs energy. The warmer the surface, the faster water evaporates. 
 2. Condensation
 As water vapor rises, it cools. Cool air holds less vapor, so the vapor changes back into tiny liquid droplets around dust particles. Billions of these droplets gather to form clouds.
 ℹ️ Everyday example Water droplets on the outside of a cold glass come from vapor in the air condensing on the cool surface. 
 3. Precipitation and Collection
 When cloud droplets merge and grow heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. This water runs over the land into streams and rivers, soaks into the ground, or collects in oceans and lakes, ready to evaporate again.
 ℹ️ Key idea The cycle has no real start or end. The same water has been recycled for billions of years. 
 Worked Example
 📋 Case Study
 Following a Raindrop
 Situation
 A puddle sits on a sunny playground after a morning storm. By afternoon, the puddle has disappeared.
 Key Data
 Morning
 Puddle 2 cm deep
 After rainfall
 Air temperature
 30 °C
 Sunny, light wind
 Afternoon
 Puddle gone
 About 6 hours later
 Analysis Questions
 1. Which stage of the water cycle made the puddle disappear?
 2. Where did the water go?
 3. What will have to happen for this water to fall as rain again?
 Show model answer ▾ 1) Evaporation: the sun's heat turned the liquid water into water vapor. 2) The water did not vanish. It is now vapor in the air. 3) The vapor will rise and cool, condense into clouds, and eventually fall again as precipitation.
