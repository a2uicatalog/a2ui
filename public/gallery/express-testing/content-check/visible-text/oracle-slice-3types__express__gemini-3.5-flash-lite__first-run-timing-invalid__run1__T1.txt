💧 The Water Cycle
 An interactive lesson on Earth's continuous movement of water
 Earth Science 
 Level 1 — Explorer 45 / 100 XP 
 Water on Earth is constantly moving. It changes form—from liquid to vapor to ice—and travels around the globe through a continuous journey known as the hydrologic cycle, or the water cycle.
 PHASE 1 
 Evaporation and Transpiration
 Heat from the sun warms up water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, causing it to turn into water vapor and rise into the atmosphere. Plants also release water vapor from their leaves through a process called transpiration.
 > Solar energy drives the upward movement of moisture into the atmosphere. 
 PHASE 2 
 Condensation
 As water vapor rises high into the atmosphere, the air cools down. This cooling causes the invisible vapor to turn back into tiny liquid water droplets or ice crystals, forming clouds and fog.
 > Cooling temperatures cause water vapor to condense into visible clouds. 
 PHASE 3 
 Precipitation and Collection
 When water droplets in clouds become too heavy to stay suspended in the air, they fall back to Earth as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. This water collects in oceans, rivers, and underground aquifers, ready to start the cycle again.
 > Gravity pulls moisture back to Earth, completing the continuous loop. 
 WORKED EXAMPLE 
 Tracing a Drop of Rain
 Imagine a single drop of water in a puddle:
1. The sun heats the puddle ( Evaporation ).
2. The vapor floats high and cools into a cloud ( Condensation ).
3. The cloud gets heavy over a mountain range, dropping snow ( Precipitation ).
4. The snow melts and flows down a stream back to the ocean ( Collection ).
 puddle -> vapor -> cloud -> snow -> stream -> ocean The complete pathway of a single water molecule.
 > Water molecules endlessly cycle through different states of matter and locations on Earth.
