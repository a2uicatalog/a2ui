💧 The Water Cycle
 How water moves between the oceans, the sky and the land, again and again
 Earth Science Sections 3 concepts
 Example 1 worked

 Water on Earth is always on the move. The water cycle has no start or end. Water changes state as it gains or loses heat energy, and the sun drives the whole process. This lesson covers the three main stages, works through one real example, and ends with a short self-check.
 Key Concepts
 DEPTH 1 
 Evaporation & Transpiration
 Heat from the sun turns liquid water in oceans, lakes and rivers into water vapour. That vapour rises into the atmosphere.
 Plants also release water vapour through tiny pores in their leaves. This is called transpiration. Together, the two processes are often called evapotranspiration.
 > Heat in → liquid becomes gas → vapour rises. 
 DEPTH 2 
 Condensation
 As water vapour rises, the air around it gets cooler. Cooler air can hold less vapour, so the vapour condenses onto tiny particles of dust or salt. This forms droplets.
 Billions of these droplets gather together and become the clouds and fog we can see.
 > Heat out → gas becomes liquid → clouds form. 
 DEPTH 3 
 Precipitation & Collection
 Droplets in a cloud bump into each other and merge. When they grow too heavy to stay in the air, they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
 The water then collects in oceans, lakes and rivers, or soaks into the ground as groundwater. Runoff carries surface water back toward the sea, ready to evaporate again.
 > Gravity brings water down. Collection closes the loop. 
 Worked Example
 WORKED EXAMPLE 
 Following one puddle after a summer storm
 A puddle forms on a playground after an afternoon storm. We can trace each stage of the cycle through it.
 1. 2 pm Sun heats puddle -> EVAPORATION (liquid -> vapour)
2. 4 pm Vapour rises & cools -> CONDENSATION (vapour -> cloud droplets)
3. 7 pm Droplets merge & grow -> PRECIPITATION (rain falls)
4. 8 pm Rain drains to a stream -> COLLECTION (runoff to river/sea) The same water molecules can travel through every stage in a single day.
 > Every puddle is a small model of the whole water cycle. 
 Check Your Understanding
 Remember
 Evaporation needs heat to be added. Condensation happens when heat is lost.
