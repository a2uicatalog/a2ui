💧 The Water Cycle
 How water moves continuously between the oceans, atmosphere, and land
 Earth Science 
 1. Evaporation and Condensation
 The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning it into water vapour. This process is called evaporation. As the vapour rises and cools, it changes back into tiny liquid droplets that gather into clouds. This change is called condensation.
 2. Precipitation and Collection
 When droplets in clouds merge and grow heavy enough, they fall as precipitation, in the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Much of this water lands in oceans, but some collects in lakes, rivers, and glaciers.
 3. Infiltration, Runoff, and Transpiration
 Some precipitation soaks into the ground, a process called infiltration, and may later seep into groundwater. Water that does not soak in flows over the surface as runoff and returns to bodies of water. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves, a process called transpiration.
 💡 Worked example A 2 litre puddle sits on a warm pavement. Heat from the sun causes some water to evaporate into the air, leaving less water on the ground. The vapour later rises, cools, and condenses into a cloud. If that cloud grows heavy, it may return the water as rain somewhere else. The same water is cycling through every stage.
